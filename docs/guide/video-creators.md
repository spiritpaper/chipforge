# For video creators

ChipForge makes retro background music and sound effects for videos, streams and
podcasts: game channels, retro reviews, pixel-art timelapses, intros and outros,
"level up" moments.

## What you can use

Everything you make in ChipForge from scratch is **yours**: songs you compose, songs
started from the built-in templates, and every sound effect. Use them in monetized
videos, no credit required. See the [FAQ](faq.md#can-i-use-what-i-make-in-a-commercial-game-or-a-monetized-video).

Converting someone else's song is different: see
[Can I convert any song and use it?](faq.md#can-i-convert-any-song-and-use-it)

## Quick recipes

**Background loop for a video.** Start from a template (**Songs → Start a new song**),
change the melody, then **Export → Audio** with **Fade out for listening** for a track
with an ending, or **Loop for games** and loop it in your editor for as long as you need.

**Sound effects for edits.** In SFX Lab: Coin (**1**) for points or money, Power-up
(**5**) for upgrades and reveals, Blip (**7**) and Select (**8**) for text popping in,
Hit (**6**) and Hurt (**9**) for fails, Explosion (**4**) for the big moment. Keep the
ones you like and download them all as one zip.

**An 8-bit version of your own intro music.** Convert it: **Lo-fi filter** keeps your
recording and crunches it; **Chip version** rebuilds it on the chip.

## Tips

- Export **WAV at 48 kHz** to match most video projects.
- Chip music is bright; keep it quieter than you think under speech (around −20 dB
  behind a voice is a good start).
- The Game Boy chip (**Chip → Game Boy**) is softer and narrower than the NES, which
  often sits better under talking.
